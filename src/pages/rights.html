Client rights

You keep your say in the care.

As a client of a licensed private home care provider, you have the right to:

  • Be treated with dignity, privacy, and respect in your own home.
  • Take part in planning your care, and to include a family member or representative if you want.
  • Be told, in language you understand, what services will be provided, by whom, and how often.
  • Refuse a treatment or a service, and to be told what that may mean.
  • Have your information kept confidential, as described in the privacy notice.
  • Raise a concern or complaint without being punished for it. Start with Tranquil, and you may also contact the state.
  • Receive care that does not discriminate because of race, color, national origin, religion, sex, age, or disability.
  • Be free from abuse, neglect, and exploitation. Tell us, or tell Adult Protective Services, if you are being harmed.

What we ask of you

Tell us about changes in your health, your medicines, and who to call. Give caregivers a safe way to enter and work. Tell us if a visit does not happen or if the care is not what the plan describes.

Georgia licenses private home care through the Department of Community Health, Healthcare Facility Regulation. The rules are published by the state. This page is a plain-language summary, not a substitute for those rules.
